Every team I have seen adopt Kanban eventually discovers the same thing: the board is the easy half. Columns, limits, swimlanes. The hard half is the feedback loop — the weekly ritual of looking at the work, not the columns.

If WIP limits never bite, if nothing changes when a card goes red, the board is furniture.

## The smell

`when:`

- Cards move right every day without the *system* changing
- Blocked items sit for weeks and nobody mentions them at standup
- The retro is the only meeting where process is discussed

One of those is fine. Two means the team is doing process theatre.

## What to do instead

Pick one constraint and actually honor it for two weeks. The smallest meaningful loop wins.
